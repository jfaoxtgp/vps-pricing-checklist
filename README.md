## What should a VPS cost per month?

Three price bands set expectations. Shared hosting generally stays below **$10/month**; a VPS runs **$20 to $100/month** depending on CPU, RAM and disk; a dedicated server usually starts at **$100+/month**. A VPS gives you a carved-out slice of a server with its own operating system, storage, RAM and monthly transfer allowance, so other customers' traffic spikes affect you far less than on shared hosting.

The managed/unmanaged split moves the price more than any hardware choice. With unmanaged (self-managed) hosting, you handle configuration, maintenance and security; the provider only hosts your data. Managed hosting hands those tasks to the provider, which buys better optimisation and support at the cost of control and a notably higher subscription fee. Liquid Web's own line shows the spread: self-managed Linux VPS from **$5.00/month**, fully managed from **$36.00/month**.

Providers define "managed" differently, so before comparing two quotes, ask whether support covers the operating system, control panel, web server, database, mail and applications. A cheap managed plan that excludes the control panel licence isn't cheap.

## How big a plan does your project need?

Start from the workload, not the price list. These four starting points are a reasonable baseline for any host:

| Workload | Starting point | What pushes requirements up |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Small business website | 2 vCPU and 4 GB RAM | Plugins, email, traffic spikes, backups |
| Several WordPress sites | 4 vCPU and 8 GB RAM | Site count, staging, caching, admin activity |
| Ecommerce store | 4+ vCPU and at least 8 GB RAM | Catalogue size, concurrent shoppers, search, checkout |
| SaaS application | Workload-specific | Databases, background jobs, APIs, user growth |
| Development environment | A smaller flexible plan | Automated builds, containers, parallel environments |

Mapped onto JustHost's list:

- **A blog or brochure site** runs fine on New Star at $3.05 or Sirius Special at $2.25. The $1.99 Promo plan works for testing and internal tools; its 512 MB of RAM and 10 Mbps port are tight for public traffic.
- **A business site with plugins and email** should follow the 2 vCPU / 4 GB rule, which means Vega Special at $6.23 or Vega at $10.75. Bright Star gives you 4 cores for $7.84 but only 4 GB of RAM.
- **Several WordPress installs or WooCommerce** need 4 cores and 8 GB: Yildun Special at $12.96 with 200 GB NVMe. RAM is the constraint that matters here, since each PHP worker and each database cache holds memory.
- **A busier store or a small SaaS app** moves to Betelgeuse Special at $21.12 (6 cores, 12 GB, 400 GB NVMe).

Buying four cores with 4 GB of RAM is the most common mismatch on this list. Core count looks better on a spec sheet, but a WordPress site with a dozen plugins will hit a memory ceiling before it saturates four cores.

## The specification that quietly limits a cheap plan

Every JustHost plan describes its port as unmetered and fair-share, then prints the equivalent monthly transfer. The Promo plan's 10 Mbps works out to about 4 TB a month; Vega's 500 Mbps to about 155 TB. The unmetered label refers to the traffic counter, not the speed.

Ten megabits per second is roughly 1.25 MB per second at peak. A page carrying 2 MB of images serves about one visitor every two seconds before anyone queues, which is fine for a private API or a staging box and frustrating for a public site with photos. If you expect real visitors, treat **100 Mbps as the practical floor** and 300 Mbps upward as comfortable.

Fair-share means other virtual machines on the same host can take bandwidth when they need it. Ask support how contention is handled if your project streams video or pushes large downloads.

## Eight things to confirm before you pay

1. **The renewal rate.** Promotional pricing without a published renewal rate is the most common complaint pattern in hosting. Get the post-discount figure in writing.
2. **What "managed" includes,** if it's offered at all. Undefined support scope means you find the boundary at the worst moment.
3. **Where backups live.** Backups stored only on the primary server disappear with the server.
4. **A published uptime commitment.** No stated figure means no recourse if the node goes down.
5. **Resource wording.** "Fair-share" ports and "unmetered" traffic need a number attached, and JustHost does print the monthly equivalent, which is more than many hosts do.
6. **Panel and licence costs.** A control panel or Windows licence that appears only at checkout changes the comparison.
7. **Server locations.** Fewer locations means higher latency for your audience. JustHost sells a São Paulo, Brazil datacenter, useful if your users are in Latin America.
8. **The migration and upgrade path.** Ask whether moving up a plan requires a full server rebuild, and whether there's a documented migration process for existing sites.

Add these to the total-cost calculation: contract length, management and support, panel and OS licensing, backup and security add-ons, and extra IP addresses or bandwidth.
